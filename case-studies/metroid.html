Context

Classic Game was the first major project in EECS 494: Game Development and Design. The task was to re-implement the first region of Metroid (Nintendo, 1986) in Unity and design a short custom level around a new mechanic, across three deliverables: a Milestone, an Alpha, and a Gold.

Timeline

  • Week 1: Milestone - First prototype of the classic level. Played the original to learn its feel, set up Unity and git, and built basic functionality for each task.
  • Week 2: Alpha - Feature-complete classic level. Cleaned up the code to cut script bloat and finished all mechanics and levels.
  • Week 3: Gold - Final classic level and custom level. Debugged, polished, and built the custom level.

Team

I worked in a team of two. For the Milestone and Alpha, the list of tasks was provided, so we split them, tracked priority and time estimates in Jira, and triaged when we fell behind. For the Gold, we first brainstormed the custom mechanic and level design and wrote our own tasks and roadmap, then followed the same process.
We worked side by side, which kept us both familiar with the whole codebase, let us help each other, and gave us shared ownership.

Custom Level Design

The Original Pitch:
Welcome Samus's newest power-up: the Transformer Tool!
With this collectible, Samus can turn her enemies into harmless blocks and those blocks back into enemies. She wields the power to reshape her surroundings to her advantage or... disadvantage.

  • Choosing the Mechanic: My goal was to create a mechanic that provided interesting decision making oppurtunities. My partner and I brainstormed through a few ideas, including mazes with breakable walls, an invisible morph ball power-up, and a bubble mechanic, before deciding on a transformation weapon, the Transformer Tool. It was feasible to build in the time we had and fit Metroid well. A big part of the original game is killing aliens to survive, but the Transformer Tool allowed us to create situations where killing everything wouldn't always be the best move.
  • Teach the Mechanic through Level Design: The challenge was to teach the player how to use the Transformer Tool, without any text guidance. To acheive this, we set up our custom level with 2 rooms. In Room 1 the player starts trapped, so that they have to learn to use the Transformer Tool to transform walls into enemies in order to escape and progress. In Room 2, we created a level that was impossible to pass by just killing or running past enemies, so as to teach the player to transform enemies back into walls in order to progress.

Results
