Context

Classic Game was the first major project in EECS 494: Game Development and Design. The task was to re-implement the first region of Metroid (Nintendo, 1986) in Unity and design a short custom level around a new mechanic, across three deliverables: a Milestone, an Alpha, and a Gold.

Timeline

  • Week 1: Milestone - First prototype of the classic level. Played the original to learn its feel, set up Unity and git, and built basic functionality for each task.
  • Week 2: Alpha - Feature-complete classic level. Cleaned up the code to cut script bloat and finished all mechanics and levels.
  • Week 3: Gold - Final classic level and custom level. Debugged, polished, and built the custom level.

Team

I worked in a team of two. For the Milestone and Alpha, the list of tasks was provided, so we split them, tracked priority and time estimates in Jira, and triaged when we fell behind. For the Gold, we first brainstormed the custom mechanic and level design and wrote our own tasks and roadmap, then followed the same process.
We worked side by side, which kept us both familiar with the whole codebase, let us help each other, and gave us shared ownership.

Reimplementation Process

We played the original game and an emulation extensively and followed online tutorials to learn how to build its mechanics. After implementing each feature, we played that part of the original and our version back to back, then fine-tuned ours until it felt as close to the original as we could get it.

Custom Level Design

The Original Pitch:
Welcome Samus's newest power-up: the Transformer Tool!
With this collectible, Samus can turn her enemies into harmless blocks and those blocks back into enemies. She wields the power to reshape her surroundings to her advantage or... disadvantage.

  • Choosing the Mechanic: The goal was to create a mechanic that created interesting decisions. My partner and I brainstormed several ideas, including mazes with breakable walls, an invisible morph ball power-up, and a bubble mechanic, then settled on a transformation weapon. It was feasible in our timeframe and fit Metroid well. The original game is about killing aliens to survive, and the Transformer Tool allowed us to create situations where killing everything isn’t always the best move.
  • Teaching the Mechanic through Level Design: The requirement was to teach the player how to use the Transformer Tool, without any text guidance. To acheive this, we set up our custom level with 2 rooms:
    • Room 1: We trapped the player in order to teach them to use the Transformer Tool to turn the blocks around them into enemies, thus creating a way out.
    • Room 2: We placed the player in a room full of floating enemies, no platforms and a floor made of lava, to teach them to transform the enemies into blocks in order to get accross. If they player kills them, they are stranded.
