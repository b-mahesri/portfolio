Context

The Rapid Prototype was the first original game assignment in EECS 494: Game Development and Design. The task was to individually design and build an original game prototype in two weeks.

Timeline

  • Week 1: Goldspike - Proved the viability of the idea by building its hardest part. Delivered a design, a project plan, and a complete, testable gameplay loop.
  • Week 2: Gold - Iterated rapidly on peer and instructor feedback, and delivered the final prototype.

Inspiration

I love driving in Karachi. It takes pure awareness, and shameless honking.

The most important unspoken rule on Karachi's roads is You Must Honk. Do it at the right moment, and watch even the sea part for you.

I wanted to recreate that familiar chaos, silliness, and thrill of my hometown in a game, and I combined it with one of its most notorious urban legends. The story goes that a bride, dressed in red, lost her groom on the night of their wedding. Distraught and vengeful, she now haunts Karsaz Road, relentlessly, and forever.

Design and Process

  • Goldspike Feature: Honk Mechanic
    Honking (space bar) makes nearby vehicles move out of the way. I built 3 vehicle types, each with its own response:
    • Moving Vehicles: move left or right
    • Parked Vehicles: move forward
    • Wrong-way Vehicles: slow down
    Other drivers only react if you're close enough for them to 'hear' you. I intended this to create a persistent challenge for the player; collisions will slow you down, but being too cautious is equally dangerous.
    The prototype has 5 sections, each a different combination of the 3 vehicle types.
  • The Bride
    Once you encounter her, the bride chases you. If she catches you, it's game over. I implemented her using Unity's NavMesh for AI pathfinding, enabling her to follow the player's bike through traffic.
    The camera follows the player, which means you can't see the bride behind you. But I designed her with her own lightsource, that glows to warn you as she gets closer. I intended these decisions to create an urgency and anxiety in the gameplay.
  • Player Guidance and Storytelling
    The game open with a tutorial and story scene. As both the controls and the story are very simple, I knew a small amount of text would be okay to use and would not overwhelm the player.
    I implemented this with a toast management system for the messages, events to trigger them, and coroutines for the story animations.

Iteration: Gold Spike to Gold

  • Bike Controls: After a collision, the bike could get stuck facing the wrong way, with no way for the player to recover. I changed the physics so the bike always realigns with the road after a collision.
  • Bride Pathfinding: The Gold Spike focused on the honk mechanic, so the bride implementation was a placeholder: a fixed object that spawned at fixed points. I replaced her with a bride built on Unity’s NavMesh, who chases the player through traffic. Much cooler.
  • Mise en Scène a.k.a. 'Juice': The Gold Spike had no lighting, sound, or set decoration, so it didn’t feel scary or like Karachi. I added lighting, sound, and story to build the mood and the tension.

Results

Instructor and Peer Feedback

  • Strengths
    • The honk mechanic is novel.
    • The game is well themed and intuitive.
    • It's fun and funny.
    • It offers interesting decisions and explores its concept well.
  • Suggested Improvements
    • Refine the placement of other vehicles and level progression.
    • Enable players to skip the tutorial.

Playthrough

Reflection

  • Bride Speed: For some players, the bride can be slow and fall too far behind, removing some of the urgency. I would make her faster, create more levels of difficulty, or have her spawn near the player several times during the game, to create more balance.
  • Collision Damage: I started implementing collision damage but didn't complete it in time. When the bike crashes into other vehicles, it slows down but suffers no further damage. Completing the damage system and an accompanying UI would raise the stakes even further, making the game more challenging and intense.
