Context

'Showcase' was the final project of EECS 494: Game Development and Design, and the Capstone project for my B.S. degree. As a team of 4, my peers and I built an original 3D game over 6 weeks in an agile, iterative process, shipping 20 minutes of gameplay designed around a novel spatial audio mechanic. The game is premised upon the player escaping a breached secret Soviet Labarotory using audio clues and cues.

Timeline

  • Week 1: Gold Spike Prove the most technically difficult element of the game and deliver a complete game loop.
  • Week 2: Milestone 1 Iterate on the Gold Spike and address playtesting feedback.
  • Week 3: Milestone 2 Iterate on Milestone 1 and address playtesting feedback.
  • Week 4: Alpha Deliver a content-complete iteration.
  • Week 5: Beta - Deliver a polished, and professional iteration.
  • Week 6: Gold - Deliver the finalised game.

Inspiration

One of my teammates, an iterarts major with a CS minor, was studying spatial audio in vietual environments. She had recently implemented spatial audio in a 3D Unity project and showed it to us. We thought it would make a novel, and immersive 3D game, that would inform interesting design decisions for our game world.

We originally started with a simple maze in which the player listens for clues, gets past obstacles and dodges enemies. Over time the concept evolved into a story based, stealth and thriller game, with spatial audio still at its heart.

Team

The 4 of us worked in an agile process, tracked in Jira. Each week followed the same cycle:

  • After each playtest, we collected feedback, planned, added, cut and assigned tasks and time estimates.
  • Mid-week, we worked independently on our assigned tasks with 1-2 virtual check-ins
  • Final 1-2 days prior to the deadline, we met and worked in person, integrated our pieces, tested and assembled the deliverable.
